The Bee Hugger Farm

Honey, harvests and happiness — a quirky six-acre Longmont farm where visitors can meet animals, explore, pick seasonal flowers and discover a wonderfully different side of our community.

A farm that invites you to “come and bee”

Along Ute Highway in Longmont is a six-acre farm filled with animals, bees, vintage tractors, seasonal flowers and the kind of unexpected details that make exploring local places fun.

The Bee Hugger Farm was founded in 2017 by LJ and Scott. Their idea is refreshingly simple: share their happy place with the community. Everyday farm visits are generally free, self-guided and designed to let visitors explore at their own pace, with optional experiences and farm products available for purchase.

Sometimes supporting local means buying honey. Sometimes it means feeding a goat, climbing an old tractor and spending an afternoon somewhere you didn’t know was right down the road.

Why The Bee Hugger is In The Spotlight

There is a lot packed into these six acres. Visitors can meet horses, peacocks, chickens, goats, a donkey and other farm animals, observe the bees, climb tractors and explore the farm’s unique vintage setting.

The farm says it is home to dozens of animals, and animal feeding is one of the simple add-on experiences available to visitors. Families are also welcome to bring a picnic and enjoy the property.

Sunflowers, pumpkins and the changing seasons

The Bee Hugger changes with the seasons. Its U-Pick sunflower field generally begins blooming in late summer, with picking offered on a donation basis when the flowers are ready. In fall, visitors can also find a seasonal pumpkin patch and hayrides.

Later in the year, the farm shifts again with Christmas trees and Santa experiences. That seasonal rhythm means a visit in September can feel completely different from a visit in December.

And then there’s the honey

Bee Hugger’s local raw honey and other bee and farm products are part of the experience. At the farm, products are available from the distinctive yellow VW bus using a self-service honor system. Their honey can also be found at a number of businesses along Colorado’s Front Range.

The farm’s connection to bees is more than a clever name. It describes itself as dedicated to providing healthy foraging habitat for pollinators, tying the sunflowers, honey and farm landscape together.

More ways to experience the farm

Beyond everyday visits, Bee Hugger offers experiences that can include pony rides, animal feeding, personalized farm visits, birthdays, private events and photography sessions. The farm has also hosted outdoor concerts and fundraisers.

Groups of 10 or more need an appointment, and photographers using the farm for sessions should arrange their visit in advance. Dogs should stay home for the safety of the farm animals.

Planning your visit

The Bee Hugger Farm is located at 12590 Ute Highway in Longmont. The farm describes its normal public schedule as 8 a.m. to sunset seven days a week, except Fridays when it closes at noon, with occasional closures for private events.

Because farm schedules, seasonal crops and special events can change, check the farm’s official calendar or social media before making a special trip.

If you’re looking for an inexpensive local outing — or simply want to discover one of Longmont’s more unusual places — this is exactly the kind of hidden-in-plain-sight experience In The Spotlight was created to share.
